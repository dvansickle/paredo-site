Looking for a simpler
RightTool alternative?

Start with what you need: a quieter QuickBooks workspace, or a broader set of workflow tools. Here’s where Paredo fits—and where RightTool does more.

By Spring Harbor Studios, the maker of Paredo · Updated October 27, 2026

A simpler alternative to RightTool—if you want fewer things on screen.

If you’re looking for a RightTool alternative because you want QuickBooks Online to feel less crowded, Paredo is worth considering. It hides supported promotions, reduces visual clutter, and gives you visibility choices on selected screens.

“Simpler” describes the scope of the product. It isn’t a finding from a usability test. We haven’t established that Paredo is faster, easier to learn, or more reliable than RightTool.

RightTool is a direct competitor with meaningful overlap. Interface enhancements and customization aren’t unique to Paredo. The useful question is which set of changes fits the work you actually do.

Where RightTool wins

RightTool has the broader published workflow toolkit. Its free edition includes navigation, favorites, search, and keyboard shortcuts. Its feature list also includes display adjustments and popup handling, so it would be misleading to describe RightTool as only adding controls.

RightTool PRO lists batch editing and deletion in the register, company switching, and tools for copying spreadsheet data into transactions. If those are the capabilities you need, Paredo won’t replace them. See RightTool’s FREE vs. PRO feature list and PRO descriptions for the scope and current availability.

RightTool also offers a free edition. Paredo has a free trial followed by a paid annual subscription. If cost is your main criterion, compare against RightTool’s free offering before deciding.

Where Paredo may fit

Paredo focuses on the presentation of selected QuickBooks Online screens. On supported views, it can tuck away recognized marketing, compact crowded headers and account cards, and adjust table readability. You can reveal optional controls or switch Paredo off for the current screen.

It may suit you if your main frustration is the amount of interface around your work and you don’t need another set of transaction tools. Paredo doesn’t post, categorize, or edit accounting records. QuickBooks remains your accounting system.

When switching doesn’t make sense

If RightTool already handles your daily workflow well, a narrower product isn’t automatically an upgrade. List the features you use before replacing anything. Paredo won’t reproduce the whole RightTool toolkit, and we haven’t verified running both extensions together.

If neither extension covers the screen or problem you have, keep the native QuickBooks layout. Paredo’s coverage varies by account and interface, and QuickBooks updates can affect compatibility.

Try the difference on an ordinary task

Choose a familiar screen, such as Home or Bank Transactions. Compare Paredo on and off. Check whether you can read the information you need, find native actions, and reveal controls you use occasionally. You don’t need to create or change accounting records just to evaluate the layout.

Use that experience to decide whether Paredo’s narrower approach earns a place in your workflow. If the missing shortcuts or batch tools matter more, RightTool may be the better fit.
