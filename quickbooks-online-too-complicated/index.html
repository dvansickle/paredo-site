Make room for your work

QuickBooks Online feels
too complicated.

When all you want to do is keep your books, a crowded screen can feel like one more task. Start by separating interface clutter from the work itself.

By Spring Harbor Studios, the maker of Paredo · Updated October 27, 2026

First, name the part that feels complicated.

“QuickBooks Online is too complicated” can mean several different things. Maybe you know the task but can’t find the control. Maybe promotions and extra panels keep pulling your attention away. Or maybe the bookkeeping decision itself is unclear.

Those problems need different remedies. A cleaner screen can help you see what’s there. It can’t tell you which accounting treatment is correct or fix a bank connection.

If the screen feels crowded, reduce the distractions.

Pick one page you use regularly. Identify the information you need for that task and the panels you rarely use. Where QuickBooks offers display or table settings, start there. Available options depend on the page and your account.

Paredo is another option for supported screens. It hides recognized promotional surfaces, trims selected headers and cards, and lets you reveal optional controls through its menu. It leaves important warnings and unrecognized dialogs available.

For example, a supported Bank Transactions view can put less emphasis on extra account cards while keeping the account picker and transaction tabs accessible. On Home, visibility choices can reduce the number of cards in view. These are display changes; they don’t change the underlying books.

If you keep losing your place, narrow the task.

Start with a concrete destination: reviewing one account’s bank transactions, finding an invoice, or reading one report. Confirm the company and account before you begin. Keep the filters and date range visible enough to check what you’re looking at.

Notice where the friction occurs. If it’s finding pages, moving among clients, or repeating navigation, a tool built around shortcuts may fit better than a display-focused extension. RightTool’s published free features include navigation, favorites, search, and keyboard shortcuts.

There’s no need to replace your accounting system just to try a different view. There’s also no need to add an extension if the native options already solve your problem.

If the accounting is unclear, get help with the decision.

A hidden promotion won’t explain an unexpected balance. A compact table won’t decide how to categorize a transaction. If the question is about what a record means or what should be entered, bring that question to your bookkeeper, accountant, or the relevant QuickBooks support resource.

Be specific about the uncertainty: “I can’t find the control” and “I don’t know which account to use” are different requests. Separating them helps you choose useful help without expecting an interface tool to solve an accounting problem.

What Paredo can—and can’t—simplify

  • It can reduce supported visual clutter. Recognized promotions, secondary controls, and selected cards can be tucked away.
  • It can make selected views more readable. Layout and table changes give the information you use more room.
  • It gives you choices. Reveal optional controls, save display preferences locally, or switch it off for the current supported screen.
  • It doesn’t automate your bookkeeping. No posting, categorizing, or editing accounting records. It doesn’t replace training, reconcile your accounts, or repair QuickBooks service problems.

Coverage includes Home, Bank Transactions, selected lists, and selected reports. It varies by account and interface. QuickBooks updates may affect compatibility, and Paredo is still evolving screen by screen.

A small way to decide whether it helps

  1. Choose one familiar screen. Use ordinary work you already need to do rather than inventing test transactions.
  2. Compare the native and simplified views. Check the company, account, balances, filters, tabs, and actions you rely on.
  3. Reveal an occasional control. Make sure the quieter view still gives you access when you need it.
  4. Keep only what helps. If a view is less useful with Paredo, switch it off for that screen.

The goal is a view that fits your work. We haven’t measured time savings or established that a cleaner layout makes bookkeeping easier for everyone. Your regular workflow is the useful test.
